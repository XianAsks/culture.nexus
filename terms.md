# Terms

## 0. Conventions

- **Tibetan** is given in Wylie transliteration, followed by a rough phonetic rendering where
  the two differ enough to matter: `thod rgal` (thögal). Wylie is authoritative; phonetics vary
  by dialect and by publisher and should never be used for lookup.
- **Sanskrit/Pali**, **Greek**, **Syriac**, **Coptic**, **Avestan** are romanized with diacritics.
- **Cross-links** use `[[term]]` per `tags.md` §4. A link to a term not yet written is a
  placeholder, not an error.
- **Translation columns matter.** Where a term's English or Italian rendering is itself part of
  the project's problem (`methods.md` §3), the renderings are listed and attributed.

## 1. Dzogchen

The vocabulary in which the project's primary text operates. Note that several of these terms
have no settled English rendering, and the variation between translators is data for H4.

### The basis and its qualities

- **`gzhi`** (zhi) — the basis, or ground. The primordial condition out of which both liberation
  and delusion arise, prior to the distinction between them. It is not a substance, a creator, or
  a self; the tradition insists it is not a thing at all, which is exactly what makes it hard to
  render in languages whose metaphysical vocabulary assumes substances. Compare [[pleroma]] and
  [[khvarenah]] with care — the resemblance is at the level of *function in a system*, not
  content, and is `#r/analogy` at best.
- **`ka dag`** (kadak) — primordial purity. The aspect of the [[gzhi]] that has never been
  obscured, regardless of what appears.
- **`lhun grub`** (lhündrub) — spontaneous presence, or self-perfection. The aspect of the
  [[gzhi]] that manifests appearance without effort or intention. Paired with [[ka dag]] as the
  two inseparable faces of the basis; the pairing is doctrinally load-bearing and any translation
  that makes them sound sequential has distorted it.
- **`rig pa`** (rigpa) — knowing awareness; the recognition of the [[gzhi]] from within. Routinely
  rendered "pure awareness," "intrinsic awareness," or simply left untranslated. Its comparison to
  Greek *gnōsis* is the substance of Conze's thesis and is `#r/analogy`; see [[gnosis]].
- **`ma rig pa`** (marigpa) — the failure of that recognition; Sanskrit *avidyā*. Not ignorance of
  facts but non-recognition of one's own condition.

### Practice

- **`khregs chod`** (trekchö) — "cutting through." The practice of resting in recognition of
  [[rig pa]] without modification.
- **`thod rgal`** (thögal) — "leaping over" or "direct crossing." The visionary practice that
  generates the [[snang ba bzhi]], typically in dark retreat or in specific light conditions.
  It is the practice basis of the [[’ja’ lus]], and the point at which Dzogchen becomes a
  discipline about *light* in a literal and phenomenal sense rather than a metaphorical one.
- **`snang ba bzhi`** — the four visions or four appearances; the staged sequence of visionary
  content arising in [[thod rgal]].
- **`mun mtshams`** — dark retreat. Extended practice in complete darkness.

### Outcomes at death

The graded family, kept distinct. See `background.md` §4 for the typology and its Christian
structural analogues.

- **`ring bsrel`** — relics; small pellet-like objects reported in the cremation remains of
  realized practitioners. `’phags pa’i ring bsrel` designates those of an ārya. This is the
  outcome that leaves physical evidence, and the nearest Tibetan analogue to Christian relic
  culture — see [[incorruptibility]].
- **`’ja’ lus`** (jalü) — rainbow body. Dissolution of the physical body into light at death,
  leaving only hair and nails. The tradition's own accounts attach a standard narrative
  apparatus: rainbows, tremors, music, fragrance, spheres of light. Type (b) in the project's
  typology, and the only one making a claim that would in principle leave an attestable absence.
- **`’od kyi sku`** / **`’od lus`** — body of light; the more general term, used across contexts
  where [[’ja’ lus]] would be too specific.
- **`’ja’ lus ’pho ba chen po`** — rainbow body of the great transfer. Transformation without
  passing through death, the adept remaining available to others; ascribed to Padmasambhava,
  Vimalamitra, and in Bön to [[Tapihritsa]]. Structurally this is the one that resembles
  [[assumption]] rather than [[resurrection]] — it skips death, which is precisely what
  resurrection does not do.
- **`thugs dam`** (tukdam) — a distinct and *adjacent* claim: remaining in meditative state after
  clinical death, without the expected decomposition. Not the rainbow body — a body that fails to
  decompose is not a body that disappears — but the same class of claim, and the only member of
  that class to have been instrumented. See `resources.md` §3.

### Textual and transmission terms

- **`gter ma`** (terma) — treasure; a text held to have been concealed, usually by Padmasambhava,
  and revealed later by a **`gter ston`** (tertön). The category is the sharpest emic/etic problem
  in Tibetan studies: the tradition dates a terma by its concealment, philology dates it by its
  language, and these are answers to different questions rather than rival answers to one
  (`methods.md` §2.5).
- **`sems sde`**, **`klong sde`**, **`man ngag sde`** — Mind Series, Space Series, Instruction
  Series; the three divisions of Dzogchen literature. The **`snying thig`** (Seminal Heart)
  belongs to the third and is where the [[’ja’ lus]] material concentrates — from the 11th
  century, which is the dating obstacle in `background.md` §7.

## 2. Bön and Zhang Zhung

- **Bön** — the Tibetan tradition presenting itself as predating the Buddhist introduction. It
  has its own Dzogchen lineage and its own rainbow-body accounts, which makes it the project's
  control case: a tradition Christianity had no route to, holding the same doctrine.
- **`sTag gzig`** (Tazig) — the land Bön names as its origin, conventionally identified with
  Iranian territory. This is a tradition's *own* claim of westward derivation, and so bears
  directly on H5 — subject to the circularity check in `methods.md` §2.6, since the
  identification with Persia may be a scholarly gloss the tradition later absorbed.
- **`’Ol mo lung ring`** (Olmo Lungring) — the mythic homeland in [[sTag gzig]].
- **Tapihritsa** — Bön master to whom the body of great transfer is ascribed; a figure of the
  [[Zhang zhung snyan rgyud]] lineage.
- **`Zhang zhung snyan rgyud`** — the Aural Transmission of Zhang Zhung; the Bön Dzogchen
  lineage text, carrying its own succession of rainbow-body attainers.

## 3. Indian Buddhist

- **`śramaṇa`** — a wandering renunciant, outside the Vedic-Brahmanical order. The category, not
  the Buddha, is the unit: he is one śramaṇa teacher among many competing ones.
- **`prajñāpāramitā`** — the perfection of wisdom; both a body of sūtras and a personified
  figure. Conze's parallel with [[Sophia]] and Ḥokhmah attaches here.
- **`bodhisattva`** — a being oriented to awakening for others' sake. Philologically important to
  this project beyond its doctrine: the word is the demonstrable loanword in the only proven
  transmission from Buddhism to Christendom, surviving as *Bodisav* → *Iodasaph* → **Josaphat**.
- **`lokottaravāda`** — the doctrine of a supramundane Buddha, held by the Mahāsāṃghika from the
  first schism. Matters because it places a transcendent Buddhology *inside* pre-Mahāyāna
  Buddhism, undercutting the picture of a sharp ascetic-to-devotional turn.
- **`āraṇyavāsin`** — forest-dweller. The term at the centre of the forest hypothesis on
  Mahāyāna origins.
- **`stūpa`** — reliquary mound. Its cult is early and monastic, which is what dismantles the
  premise that mainstream Buddhism was austerely non-devotional.

## 4. Chan / Zen

- **`bSam gtan mig sgron`** — *Lamp for the Eye of Contemplation*, Nubchen Sangye Yeshe, 10th c.
  The primary witness distinguishing Chan from Dzogchen at the moment the two were in contact.
- **Moheyan** — the Chinese Chan master of the Samye debate narrative, in which he is defeated by
  Kamalaśīla. The narrative is itself a source-critical problem, not a neutral record.

## 5. Christian — Greek and Latin

- **`sōma pneumatikon`** (σῶμα πνευματικόν) — "spiritual body," 1 Cor 15:44, opposed to
  `sōma psychikon`. Paul's term for the resurrected body: sown perishable, raised imperishable,
  yet still a *body*. The most philosophically precise Christian statement on the question, and
  the most comparable to [[’od kyi sku]] — though the comparison must survive the observation
  that Paul is insisting on continuity of body where Dzogchen describes its dissolution.
- **`anastasis`** (ἀνάστασις) — resurrection; literally "standing up again." Presupposes death.
- **`analēmpsis`** / **assumption** — being taken up without dying; the Enoch and Elijah pattern.
  The structural analogue to [[’ja’ lus ’pho ba chen po]].
- **`metamorphōsis`** (μεταμόρφωσις) — the Transfiguration; a *living* body becoming luminous and
  seen to be so. Structurally the closest Christian event to [[thod rgal]]'s visionary light, and
  a better comparison target than the empty tomb.
- **`entos hymōn`** (ἐντὸς ὑμῶν) — Luke 17:21, "the kingdom of God is `entos hymōn`." Genuinely
  ambiguous: *entos* normally means "within," but with a plural it can mean "among, in the midst
  of." The choice between "within you" and "among you" decides whether the verse supports an
  interiorized kingdom, and translators' reasons for choosing are partly theological. A
  transmission-distortion case (`methods.md` §3) occurring inside the canonical text itself.
- **`apokatastasis`** (ἀποκατάστασις) — universal restoration; the doctrine that all will
  finally be saved. Associated with Origen and Gregory of Nyssa, i.e. 3rd–4th century, which is
  the relevant fact when asking whether the historical Jesus taught it.
- **docetism** — the position that Christ only *appeared* to have a body and to suffer. Declared
  heretical. Its significance here is structural: it is the Christian option in which no real
  death occurs, and orthodoxy defined itself partly by rejecting it.
- **incorruptibility** — the non-decay of a saint's body, treated as an index of sanctity. The
  Christian practice most genuinely parallel to [[ring bsrel]] and to [[thugs dam]] — less
  glamorous than the resurrection parallel and probably more real.

## 6. Syriac

- **`ʾestalyā d-šubḥā`** — the "robe of glory." The Syriac motif of a garment of light lost at
  the Fall and recovered in salvation, developed richly by Ephrem and central to the
  *Hymn of the Pearl*. Its family resemblance to [[khvarenah]] is the Christian end of H5.

## 7. "Gnostic" and Coptic

- **`gnōsis`** (γνῶσις) — salvific knowledge, as distinct from propositional knowledge or faith.
  Conze compared Mahāyāna to *gnosis* in this sense and explicitly not to the Gnostics as a social
  group; the distinction is routinely lost in later citation. Compare [[rig pa]] — `#r/analogy`.
- **`plērōma`** (πλήρωμα) — fullness; the divine totality from which lower orders emanate.
- **Sophia** (Σοφία) — Wisdom, personified; in several Sethian and Valentinian texts the figure
  whose error produces the material world. The comparison to [[prajñāpāramitā]] is Conze's most
  substantive observation.
- **"Gnosticism"** — a modern scholarly category, built substantially out of heresiological
  polemic and challenged by Williams and King. Retained in this project only in scare quotes.

## 8. Iranian and Avestan

- **`khvarenah`** (Avestan; Middle Persian **`farr`**, **`farrah`**) — divine glory, radiance,
  kingly fortune. An Iranian concept of a luminous quality attaching to legitimate rule and to
  divine beings. In Sasanian art it is rendered as a **circular halo around the ruler's head**,
  and this is the hinge of H5: the same visual convention appears in Gandhāran Buddhist art from
  the 1st century CE and in Christian art from the 4th, which suggests common inheritance rather
  than borrowing between the latter two.
- **`prabhāmaṇḍala`** (Sanskrit) — the Buddhist nimbus or light-disc. Appears prominently in
  Kushan-period Gandhāran schist.
- **nimbus** — the general art-historical term for the disc or ring of light behind a head. Kept
  separate from [[khvarenah]] and [[prabhāmaṇḍala]] deliberately: the *convention* is one thing,
  and each tradition's account of what it signifies is another. Conflating them is how an
  iconographic homology gets mistaken for a doctrinal genealogy.

## 9. Central Asian philology

- **Gāndhārī** — the Middle Indo-Aryan language of Gandhāra, written in **Kharoṣṭhī**. The
  language of the earliest surviving Buddhist manuscripts.
- **Bactrian** — an Iranian language written, unusually, in Greek script; the Kushan chancery
  language. The script choice is itself evidence of Hellenistic administrative inheritance.
- **Sogdian** — the Iranian lingua franca of Silk Road commerce; also the language of Christian
  texts from Bulayïq, written in Syriac script.
- **Tocharian** — the Indo-European languages of the Tarim Basin, whose speakers were long assumed
  to be migrants until ancient DNA complicated the picture.
- **Jingjiao** (景教) — "Luminous Teaching"; the Chinese name for the Church of the East. Note the
  light-vocabulary in the name itself, which is suggestive and proves nothing.

## 10. Method terms

- **emic / etic** — categories internal to a system as its participants operate them, versus
  categories imposed from outside for cross-system comparison. See `methods.md` §2.

  **Where the words come from, and how to hold on to them.** Pike (1954) made the pair by cutting
  *phon-* off **phonEMIC** and **phonETIC**.

  - *Phonemic* describes the sound distinctions that carry meaning **inside** one language.
    English /l/ and /r/ are separate phonemes — "light" and "right" are different words. Japanese
    draws no such line: the acoustic difference is there, but it carries no information for a
    speaker.
  - *Phonetic* describes the full range of sound a human can produce, recorded from **outside** any
    particular language in a universal notation.

  So **emic** = the distinctions that mean something within the system; **etic** = the
  distinctions an outside observer can register with a general-purpose scheme. If the pair slips,
  recover it by putting *phon-* back on the front.

  Letter-hooks on the one letter that differs. e**m**ic — **m**eaning, what it **m**eans to
  **m**embers. e**t**ic — the **t**able: the phonetic alphabet is a physical chart, built to work
  for every language and belonging to none, which is exactly what an etic scheme is.

  **Not insider versus outsider.** This is the common error, and the reason `methods.md` §2 flags
  it. An anthropologist who has never joined a tradition can write an emic description, by
  rendering the tradition's own categories faithfully. A lifelong practitioner can write an etic
  one, by applying a comparative grid. The distinction is about **which categories are being
  used**, not about who is using them.
- **genealogy / analogy / homology** — a claim of historical descent; a claim of structural
  similarity without contact; a claim of shared descent from a third source. The distinction the
  `#r/` facet enforces, and the one whose collapse J. Z. Smith diagnoses.
- **wanderwort** — a word that spreads across unrelated language families with the thing it names,
  so that it cannot be assigned to any one family's inheritance. `*medʰu` (honey/mead) reaching as
  far as Chinese is the classic case. Wanderwörter are the linguistic fingerprint of the *market*
  channel; inherited kinship and nature vocabulary is the fingerprint of the *household* channel.
- **pidgin / creole** — a pidgin is a contact language with no native speakers, restricted to
  limited domains and improvised by adults across codes. A **creole** arises when children acquire
  a pidgin natively and it develops full grammar. The transition requires households containing
  children, which makes it the precise linguistic marker of the assembly/assimilation threshold
  (`background.md` §5.4). Gullah is the type specimen.
- **borrowing scale** (Thomason & Kaufman) — the graded relationship between contact intensity and
  what a language borrows. Lexical borrowing alone indicates casual contact; **structural and
  grammatical** borrowing indicates sustained, household-level contact. Gives Q8a a measurable
  proxy: degree of structural interference, not number of loanwords.
- **substrate / adstrate / superstrate** — an absorbed prior language, a neighbouring language of
  comparable status, a socially dominant one. The three leave different traces, and which one a
  contact zone shows is evidence about its social structure, not only its linguistics.
- **contact zone** — a place and period in which distinct traditions are co-present. Co-presence is
  the *minimum* condition and establishes only opportunity — rung 5 on the `methods.md` §5 ladder.
  Distinguish sharply from a generative nexus: Dunhuang was an exceptional contact zone and produced
  a library rather than a synthesis.
- **transmitter / innovator** — the distinction Q8 turns on. A network that carries three religions
  across a continent without altering any of them is transmitting, not innovating; the Sogdian case
  is the type specimen. Conflating the two inflates every contact zone into a creative one.
- **contingency / convergence** — the paleobiological form of the [[genealogy]]/[[analogy]]
  distinction. Contingency (Gould) holds that outcomes are path-dependent and history is
  irreproducible; convergence (Conway Morris) holds that similar constraints independently produce
  similar solutions. Forty years of argument with a developed sense of what counts as evidence on
  each side — worth mining for criteria, per `resources.md` §14.5.
- **analog vs digital channel** — genes copy discretely over a four-letter alphabet with dedicated
  error correction; culture transmits through perception and re-enactment of continuous signals, so
  variation is **deformation rather than substitution** and there is no proofreading. Analog does not
  mean unconstrained: acquisition biases act as strong structural attractors, which is why creoles
  regularise. The disanalogy to carry, in place of the unsafe "cultural transmission is Lamarckian."
  See `methods.md` §1.2a.
- **cultural attractor** — a variant that reconstruction reliably converges on, so that transmission
  is *transformation toward* it rather than copying of it (Sperber; Claidière & Sperber). The
  developed form of the analog-channel position, and a direct rival to memetics, which requires
  faithful replication.
- **ghost lineage** — a branch inferred to have existed from the pattern of survivors, with no direct
  record of its own. The textual analogue is a lost recension known only because two surviving
  witnesses share errors neither could have got from the other.
- **Signor–Lipps effect** — the last surviving record of something precedes its actual end, because
  sampling is incomplete; consequently abrupt endings look gradual. Paleobiology's name for a bias
  this project meets constantly under other names. See [[termination bias]], [[archival silence]].
- **stemmatics / stemma / phylomemetics** — **stemmatics** is the method textual critics use to
  work out how surviving manuscript copies of a text are related, and from that to reconstruct the
  lost original. Its product is a **stemma**, a copying-tree of the surviving **witnesses** down
  from a reconstructed **archetype**. Also called stemmatology, or Lachmann's method.

  **The principle that makes it work, and the reason it matters to this project: only shared
  *errors* are informative.** Two manuscripts agreeing on a correct reading tells you nothing —
  either could have got it right independently, or from anywhere. Two manuscripts sharing the same
  *distinctive mistake* is strong evidence of common descent, because the same error is unlikely to
  arise twice by chance. Errors that group witnesses together are **conjunctive**; errors present in
  one and absent from another prove the second is not descended from the first, and are
  **separative**.

  That is `methods.md` §5 rung 3 — shared arbitrary detail, non-obvious, with no functional reason
  to arise twice — as a mature and formalised discipline. A scribal slip is an arbitrary shared
  detail in its purest form. The project is making by hand an inference textual criticism has been
  refining since the nineteenth century.

  **Phylomemetics** applies phylogenetic inference to the same problem. Formally it is
  reconstructing a species tree from extant taxa, with the same survivorship conditioning, and it
  meets the same obstacle: **contamination**, where a scribe consults two exemplars, produces
  reticulation, so the true structure is a network rather than a tree. That is horizontal gene
  transfer under another name.

  **What a stemma measures that this project wants**: *where* variation enters a copying tradition —
  whether innovations cluster on a few scribes or accumulate broadly across all of them. See
  `background.md` §5.7. See [[survivorship]], [[frozen accident]].
- **frozen accident** — an outcome that was contingent when it happened but became unrevisable once
  everything downstream came to depend on it. In the Cambrian case, conserved gene-regulatory-network
  kernels are the standard account of why body plans stopped changing. The reason radiations are rare
  is not that the conditions are rare but that **the design space stays occupied**.
- **memoryless (constant hazard)** — a lifetime distribution in which the probability of ending in
  the next interval does not depend on how long the thing has already lasted; equivalently, an
  exponential lifespan distribution. Van Valen (1973) argued this for taxa; Arbesman (2011) found it
  for empires. Its consequence here is deflationary: **age carries no information about fragility**,
  so "this polity lasted 400 years" is not evidence of robustness, and "they all ended" is not
  evidence of anything. See [[termination bias]] for what survives the deflation.
- **termination bias** — the over-representation in the record of phenomena that ended dramatically,
  because catastrophe generates archives (refugees, polemics, inventories, destruction layers, sealed
  caves) while continuity generates few. The mirror of [[archival silence]]: one biases downward what
  was never written, the other biases upward what stopped loudly.
- **archival silence** — the systematic absence from a record of what the record's makers did not
  write about. Because it is directional rather than random, it cannot be treated as noise: it
  biases every estimate of the silenced phenomenon downward. The household transmission channel is
  the standing example here, since it runs through caregivers who do not write. See `methods.md` §7.1.
- **sampling on the dependent variable** — selecting cases *because* they exhibit the outcome, then
  enumerating what they share. The resulting list of "preconditions" may equally describe cases that
  produced nothing. The standing hazard of Q8a and deflator 7 in `methods.md` §6; the remedy is
  matched negative cases, not abandonment of the question.
- **criterion of embarrassment** — a historical-Jesus criterion: material a tradition would not
  have invented because it is awkward for it is more likely to be historical. Relevant here
  because it is one of the few tools that gets purchase on the [[John the Baptist]] question.

## 11. Open problems

- **"Dzogchen Kazhag Rangdrol"** — a terma cycle of [[Nyagla Pema Dündul]] as transcribed in
  Norbu's *Talks in California, USA 1982*. The Wylie is **not recovered**. The transcription
  passed through Norbu's spoken, Italian-accented Tibetan, an English transcriber's ear, and
  print. Candidates include `dka’ zhag rang grol` and `bka’ bzhag rang grol`. This is
  `methods.md` §3.3's worked micro-case: a closed instance of the whole transmission problem,
  at a scale where the answer is checkable.
- **`sTag gzig` ↔ Iran** — is the identification the tradition's own or a scholarly gloss?
  H5 cannot rest on the Bön convergence until this is settled.
